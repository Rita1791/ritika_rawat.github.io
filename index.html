About

Overview

I am an early-career computational biology researcher working across comparative genomics, evolutionary cancer biology and genomic-variation analysis. My flagship study asks whether recurrent human TP53 cancer hotspots are unusually constrained across mammalian evolution.

Alongside this work, I design reproducible FASTQ-to-VCF workflows and evaluate them against Genome in a Bottle reference data. My experimental training in pharmaceutical analytical sciences helps me distinguish computational prioritization from functional validation and frame realistic follow-up experiments.

I am preparing for PhD research in comparative genomics, cancer genomics or computational genomics in Switzerland and Europe.

01 · Selected research

Questions first. Methods second. Claims bounded by evidence.

Three projects trace a coherent progression from comparative TP53 analysis to residue-level evolutionary statistics and benchmark-aware genomic variation analysis.

Flagship research · Comparative cancer genomics

Evolutionary constraint of TP53 mutation hotspots across mammals

01
Central question

Do recurrent human cancer-associated TP53 hotspots occur at residues that are unusually constrained across mammalian evolution?

Study design

Accession-level sequence curation, multiple sequence alignment, human-coordinate residue mapping, conservation and entropy analysis, hotspot-control comparison, permutation testing, phylogenetic context and structural interpretation.

Interpretive boundary

Conservation identifies candidate functional constraint. It does not establish molecular mechanism, cancer resistance or cross-species functional equivalence.
